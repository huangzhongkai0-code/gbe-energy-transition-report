## Executive Summary

**Question:** What operating and financing model should Great British Energy adopt to support the UK energy transition without creating excessive fiscal or project risk?

**Approach:** Compare EDF, Vattenfall and Ørsted across ownership, capital intensity, financial resilience, governance and transition strategy, then translate the lessons into recommendations for GBE.

**Conclusion:** GBE should combine initial public capital with green bonds and project-level public-private partnerships, prioritise offshore wind, grid storage and hydrogen, and scale projects in stages with explicit capital-discipline and affordability safeguards.

## Decision Scorecard

The enhancement converts the qualitative comparison into an explicit decision aid. Scores run from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong); weights reflect the policy objective and are transparent assumptions rather than measured outcomes.

| Financing route | Fiscal resilience 25% | Energy security 25% | Delivery speed 20% | Affordability 15% | Governance 15% | Weighted score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The recommended solution is not a winner-takes-all route: use public seed capital for mandate alignment, green bonds for scalable finance and PPPs for project-level risk sharing.

## Project Background

Set against the UK government's real policy initiative to establish **Great British Energy (GBE)** — a publicly owned clean energy company — this project simulates the role of policy advisor to the UK Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero. The report researches GBE's business model and operating structure by benchmarking it against three established European energy companies, drawing lessons for GBE's financing, pricing, investment, and risk strategy.

## Methodology

- Traced the historical evolution of the UK energy system, from the 1980s privatization era through 1990s–2000s market liberalization, to the establishment of GBE
- Benchmarked **EDF (France), Vattenfall (Sweden), and Ørsted (Denmark)** across financial performance (revenue, EBITDA, net financial debt, net profit, capital structure), governance, and social/environmental responsibility
- Synthesized findings into concrete policy recommendations across financing model, pricing strategy, and investment priorities

## Key Findings

**Verified 2024 reporting snapshots:**

| Company | Officially reported 2024 snapshot | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| EDF | Sales €118.7bn; EBITDA €36.5bn; net income €11.4bn; net financial debt €54.3bn; NFD/EBITDA 1.49x | Strong operating recovery, but a capital-intensive balance sheet and major-project execution risk remain central lessons for GBE. |
| Vattenfall | Net sales SEK 245.57bn; underlying operating profit SEK 19.83bn; profit for the period SEK 33.38bn | A diversified state-owned utility can combine commercial operation with transition investment, although reported earnings remain exposed to disposals and power-price conditions. |
| Ørsted | 2024 annual reporting reviewed qualitatively because its DKK measures and partnership/impairment effects are not directly comparable with EDF and Vattenfall metrics | Offshore-wind expertise is strategically relevant, but rapid expansion and project impairments underline the need for disciplined phasing and risk allocation. |

Sources: [EDF 2024 annual results](https://www.edf.fr/en/the-edf-group/dedicated-sections/journalists/all-press-releases/2024-annual-results), [Vattenfall 2024 year-end report](https://group.vattenfall.com/press-and-media/pressreleases/2025/vattenfall--year-end-report-2024), and [Ørsted 2024 annual reporting](https://orsted.com/en/investors/ir-material/annual-reporting-2024).

> Comparability note: the companies report in different currencies and use different operating-profit definitions. The table therefore presents verified company-reported snapshots rather than forcing a potentially misleading euro conversion or like-for-like EBITDA comparison.

- **EDF**: strong nuclear asset base, but weighed down by high leverage and repeated cost overruns/delays on projects like Hinkley Point C
- **Vattenfall**: stable, diversified renewables portfolio (wind/hydro), but exposed to energy price volatility
- **Ørsted**: technology leadership in offshore wind, but rapid expansion has strained capital discipline and profitability

## Policy Recommendations for GBE

1. **Financing** — diversify beyond government funding using green bonds and public-private partnerships (PPP), avoiding over-reliance on state capital
2. **Pricing** — adopt a dynamic price cap mechanism, with targeted subsidies for low-income households to address fuel poverty
3. **Investment priorities** — offshore wind, grid-scale energy storage, and hydrogen as core growth areas
4. **Risks to avoid** — Ørsted's overly rapid expansion, and EDF's unresolved nuclear waste disposal liability
